<Section anchor="start" title="Getting started">
<Faq q="I have dozens of tabs open. Can I save them all at once?">Yes. Choose New → Sweep open tabs. Pick tabs one by one, by site, or all of them, choose or create a collection, and save. They close as they’re saved (you can turn that off), and Undo brings them back.</Faq>
<Faq q="Can I open a whole collection at once?">Yes. Open the collection and press Open all. The links open in this window, a new window or a tab group named after the collection, and pages already open are skipped. Mark a collection as a launcher and it appears at the top of the popup for one-click opening.</Faq>
<Faq q="Can I dictate notes?">Yes. Press the mic button on any note or description and speak; say “comma”, “full stop” or “new line” for punctuation. Chrome’s speech service, run by Google, turns the audio into text. We never receive the audio.</Faq>
<Faq q="What is Jupiter Jots?">A bookmark manager for Chrome that remembers why you saved each page. Save the page you are on in one click, add a note for future you, sort it into collections and tags, and search it all later. It is free and needs no account.</Faq>
<Faq q="Which browsers does it work in?">Chrome. Other Chromium browsers that install extensions from the Chrome Web Store may work, but Jupiter Jots is built and tested on Chrome.</Faq>
<Faq q="Where do I find it after installing?">Click the puzzle piece in Chrome’s toolbar and pin Jupiter Jots. Its button opens the popup. From there you can open the side panel, or the full-screen library for sorting and cleaning up. All three are the same app.</Faq>
<Faq q="How do I save a page?">Open the popup and choose Save, use Save this page in the side panel, right-click a page or link and choose Save to Jupiter Jots, or press Alt+Shift+S. If you saved the page before, Jupiter Jots tells you instead of making a duplicate.</Faq>
<Faq q="What is the difference between collections and tags?">A bookmark lives in one collection, like a folder, and collections can sit inside other collections as deep as you like. Tags cut across collections: one bookmark can have many. Use collections for where things belong and tags for what they are about.</Faq>
<Faq q="Does it work offline?">Yes. Your library is stored in your browser, so you can browse, search and organise it without a connection. Opening a saved page still needs the internet.</Faq>
</Section>

<Section anchor="privacy" title="Your data and privacy">
<Faq q="Where is my data stored?">In your browser’s own database (IndexedDB) on this computer. That includes bookmarks, notes, tags, collections, settings and the activity log used for Insights. None of it is sent to us. The only exception is a share you choose to make.</Faq>
<Faq q="Do you collect anything or track me?">Not from your library. There is no account, no analytics, no tracking and no advertising. If you share or comment, what you share or write is stored on our server. Nothing is sold. The privacy policy has the details, including the few things the extension loads from the web, such as preview images.</Faq>
<Faq q="Why does it ask for permission to read pages?">To save a page, Jupiter Jots reads its address, title, description and preview image, only when you save it. Access to all sites is optional and off by default; it lets the side panel read descriptions too. Your Chrome bookmarks are read only when you choose to import them.</Faq>
<Faq q="What happens when I share?">The share is stored in the extension and on our server, so its link works and your other devices can manage it. An unlisted or public share is readable by anyone with the link. A password-protected one is encrypted in your browser with its password, so our server can’t read its contents or comments. It can see the title, your curator name, the item counts, the share’s settings, and which item each comment is on. Comments are stored on our server too. A shared note is always included in full; Include my notes only decides whether your bookmark and collection notes go with it. Linking a collection or graph to a shared note shares its contents too. When someone opens a share, their browser loads the bookmarks’ site icons and preview images straight from those sites, as any web page would. You can delete a share from the extension, and its comments go with it. There is no account: a 12-character code made on your device identifies you, and we can’t recover it. The [privacy policy](/privacy#sharing) has the details.</Faq>
<Faq q="What do Unlisted, Public and Password protected mean?">They decide who can reach a share. **Unlisted** is the default: only people you give the link to can open it. It isn’t listed anywhere, and search engines are asked not to index it. **Public**: anyone with the link can open it, and it is also listed on [Explore](/explore) and may appear in search engines, so strangers can find it. **Password protected**: people need the link and the password, and the contents and comments are encrypted in your browser, so our server can’t read them. You can switch a share between unlisted and public later; a password-protected share stays that way. Shares made before Public existed are unlisted.</Faq>
<Faq q="What does Insights record, and can I turn it off?">Insights is built from a log of when you open or save a bookmark and from where. The log stays on your device. You can pause or clear it in Settings at any time.</Faq>
<Faq q="What happens if I uninstall?">Your library is deleted with the extension, and we have no copy. Export or back up first if you might want it again.</Faq>
<Faq q="Is my library synced between computers?">Not yet. Each computer has its own library. To move it, export a backup on one and import it on the other.</Faq>
</Section>

<Section anchor="import" title="Import and export">
<Faq q="Can I bring my Chrome bookmarks?">Yes. On the Import & export page choose Import from Chrome, allow access when Chrome asks, and pick the folders you want. They go into a collection you name, each folder becomes a collection inside it, and the nesting is kept. Your Chrome bookmarks are never changed.</Faq>
<Faq q="What about Firefox, Safari, Edge, Raindrop or Pocket?">Export a bookmarks HTML file from any browser and import it. Jupiter Jots also reads CSV files from Raindrop, Pocket or a spreadsheet, and its own JSON backups.</Faq>
<Faq q="Will importing create duplicates?">No. Pages you already saved are skipped. If an import isn’t what you wanted, you can undo the whole import in one step.</Faq>
<Faq q="How do I export or back up?">On the Import & export page, export everything or a single collection as JSON, browser bookmarks HTML or CSV. JSON keeps everything, including notes and tags, and is the best format for a backup.</Faq>
</Section>

<Section anchor="shortcuts" title="Shortcuts">
<Faq q="What is the shortcut to save a page?">Alt+Shift+S saves the page you are on. You can change it on chrome://extensions/shortcuts.</Faq>
<Faq q="Can a shortcut open a site I use every day?">Yes, up to nine. In Settings, choose the site for each shortcut, then set its keys on chrome://extensions/shortcuts. If the site is already open, the shortcut switches to that tab instead of opening another.</Faq>
<Faq q="Why can’t I set the keys inside Jupiter Jots?">Chrome only lets you set extension keys on its own shortcuts page. Jupiter Jots has a button that takes you straight there.</Faq>
</Section>

<Section anchor="soon" title="Coming soon" note="These are planned and not in the extension yet.">
<Faq q="Will there be sync between devices?">It is planned, and it will be optional. Until you turn it on, your library stays only on your device, as it does now.</Faq>
<Faq q="Are there mobile apps?">Phone apps are planned, so you can save from your phone’s share menu and read later. They will arrive with sync.</Faq>
<Faq q="Can it remind me to read something?">Reminders are planned: pick a time to come back to a bookmark, and Jupiter Jots will remind you.</Faq>
<Faq q="What is Ask your library?">A planned AI assistant that works on what you saved: answering questions, summarising your links on a topic, suggesting tags and organising things. Any change it proposes is shown to you first and applied only when you confirm. It sees a page’s title, address and description and your own notes, never the page itself, and it never sees notes you hide from AI or lock. It does nothing until you add a key.</Faq>
<Faq q="Whose AI does it use, and who sees my keys?">Your own. You bring a key from the provider you choose (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or OpenRouter, some of whose models are free) and add it in Settings → AI providers. The key is stored only in this browser: it is never sent to Jupiter Jots, synced, or put in a backup. Requests go straight from your browser to that provider, so our server never sees your keys, your questions or the answers. What the provider receives is your question plus the library items the assistant reads to answer it, under that provider’s own privacy terms.</Faq>
</Section>
