<Summary>

- Your library stays in your browser on this device. We never receive it, except what you put in a share.
- The one exception is a share you choose to make: it is stored on our server so its link works.
- Private shares are encrypted in your browser. We can’t read their contents or comments.
- There is no account and no email. No analytics, no advertising and no tracking. Nothing is sold.
- Share pages set one functional cookie, `jj_visitor`, and no other cookies. They load site icons and preview images straight from the sites that host them.
- Pages are read only when you save them. Chrome bookmarks are read only when you import them.
- Uninstalling deletes your library. Export a backup first if you want to keep it.

</Summary>

<Section anchor="who" title="Who this covers">
<Prose>

This policy covers the Jupiter Jots extension for Chrome, version 2.0.0 and later, and the share pages on this website that its share links open, including for people who visit or comment on them. Jupiter Jots is built and maintained by an independent developer, called “we” and “us” here.

</Prose>
</Section>

<Section anchor="collect" title="What we collect">
<Prose>

**From your library, nothing.** Jupiter Jots has no account and asks for no email. The extension contains no analytics, telemetry, advertising or crash-reporting code, and it does not track the sites you visit.

**From shares and share pages:** what you share, comments people post, and a hashed visitor key. See [When you share](#sharing) and [Visiting and commenting](#visitors).

We never see your bookmarks, notes, tags, collections, settings or how you use the extension, unless you put them in a share. Sharing is the only feature that sends anything to our server, and only what you choose to share, as described in [When you share](#sharing). We do not sell or hand on anything we receive.

</Prose>
</Section>

<Section anchor="stored" title="What is stored on your device">
<Prose>

Your library lives in your browser’s own database (IndexedDB), inside your Chrome profile on this computer. It holds:

- **Bookmarks:** each page’s address and title, its description and preview image address if the page provides them, any text you had selected, and the date you saved it.
- **Notes, tags and collections**, including the icon, emoji or colour you give a collection.
- **Settings**, such as your theme, default collection and the sites your keyboard shortcuts open.
- **An activity log** of when you open or save a bookmark, and from where (popup, side panel, full screen or a shortcut). It powers the Insights page. You can pause or clear it in Settings.

Jupiter Jots asks Chrome for unlimited local storage so a large library is not trimmed. That storage never leaves your device.

</Prose>
</Section>

<Section anchor="reads" title="What the extension reads">
<Prose>

Jupiter Jots reads a page only when you ask it to save something.

- **When you open the popup**, it reads the address and title of the tab you are on, so it can save it and tell you if it is already saved.
- **When you save a page**, a one-off script runs in that tab to read the page’s own description and preview image from its meta tags, and any text you have selected. It reads nothing else.
- **While the side panel or full-screen library is open**, it sees the address and title of the tab in front, to offer Save this page. These are compared on your device and kept only if you save the page.
- **Read descriptions from any tab** is an optional setting, off by default. If you turn it on, Chrome asks you to allow access to all sites, so the side panel and full screen can read the description of the page in front when you save it. You can turn it off in Settings.
- **Your Chrome bookmarks** are read only when you choose Import from Chrome, after Chrome asks your permission. Jupiter Jots copies the folders you pick and never changes your Chrome bookmarks.

</Prose>
</Section>

<Section anchor="network" title="What leaves your device">
<Prose>

Your library is never sent to us. Jupiter Jots makes only the requests any web page makes to show pictures, plus one request to Google if you choose a site logo for a collection, and the requests a share needs, described in the next sections.

</Prose>
<Defs>
<Def term="Preview images">A bookmark’s picture is the page’s own preview image, loaded from that site when the bookmark is shown.</Def>
<Def term="Site icons">The small icon next to a bookmark is the one the site declares, loaded from that site, or Chrome’s own saved copy of it.</Def>
<Def term="Collection logos">Only if you pick Logo as a collection’s icon and type a domain. The logo is loaded from Google’s favicon service, which tells Google the domain you typed.</Def>
<Def term="Opening a bookmark">Opens the site in a tab, exactly as clicking a link would.</Def>
<Def term="Speak to type">Only while the mic button on a note is lit. Chrome’s built-in speech recognition sends the audio to Google’s speech service, which returns the text; Google’s privacy policy covers that audio. We never receive it, and the text stays in your note on your device.</Def>
<Def term="Shares">Only when you create, update or manage a share. What is sent is described below.</Def>
</Defs>
</Section>

<Section anchor="sharing" title="When you share">
<Prose>

Your bookmarks stay on your device. A **share** is the exception. When you create one, it is stored in two places: in your own space in the extension, and on Jupiter Jots servers, so it can be reached by its link and by your other devices. If you keep a share live, changes to the bookmarks in it are uploaded as you make them.

</Prose>
<Defs>
<Def term="Public shares">Their contents are stored readable on our server and shown to anyone with the link: the title and intro, your curator name, and the bookmarks, collections and graphs you add. For each bookmark that is its address, title, site, tags, note (unless you leave notes out), whether you have opened it, and the addresses of its site icon and preview image. For each collection it is its name, colour, icon and description (unless you leave notes out). A logo you uploaded yourself is not included. A collection includes the collections nested inside it. A shared note includes what it links to: a linked bookmark, and a linked collection or graph with its contents.</Def>
<Def term="Private shares">Their contents and their comments are encrypted in your browser with the share password before they leave it. Our server can’t read its contents or comments. It can see the title, your curator name and the item counts (such as “1 graph · 2 collections”) shown on the lock screen, the share’s settings, and which item each comment is on. It also stores the values your browser needs to turn the password into a key. The password never reaches us in a form we can read, so we can’t recover it.</Def>
<Def term="Settings">For every share we also store its settings (public or private, comment mode, expiry, view limit, keep live), a view count, when it was created, changed or revoked, and a fingerprint of its contents that tells your devices when it has changed.</Def>
<Def term="Your list of shares">So your other devices can manage a share, the extension also stores with it the list of what is in it and, for a private share, its password. Both are encrypted on your device with a key from your code that never leaves it, so we can’t read them.</Def>
</Defs>
</Section>

<Section anchor="code" title="Your code">
<Prose>

Sharing needs no email and no account. Your identity is a **12-character code** generated on your device. From it, your device derives a key (with Argon2id) that it sends to manage your shares. Our server stores only a keyed hash (HMAC) of that key, with when it was first and last used. It can recognise your requests, but it can’t work out the key or recover the code.

Enter the same code on another device to manage your shares there. Keep it somewhere safe. **If you lose the code, you lose control of your existing shares**: they keep working until they expire, but you can no longer change or revoke them yourself.

</Prose>
</Section>

<Section anchor="visitors" title="Visiting and commenting on a share">
<Prose>

Share pages have no analytics, advertising or tracking. This is what they store and send.

</Prose>
<Defs>
<Def term="Visitor key">

The first time you open a share page, your browser makes a random key. It is kept in your browser’s local storage and in one first-party cookie, `jj_visitor`, which lasts a year. It is sent to our server with your requests, and the server stores it only as a hash (SHA-256). It is used to:

- count a view, usually once per visitor per day
- record your votes and reports, so each counts once
- show you your own comments while they wait for approval
- limit how fast comments and votes can be sent

It is not used for advertising and does not follow you to other sites.

</Def>
<Def term="Your name">If you comment, the name you type is remembered in your browser on that device, so you don’t have to type it again. It is not remembered on private shares.</Def>
<Def term="Comments">

Comments and replies are stored on our server, with the hashed visitor key. Each one holds:

- your name, what you write, and when
- the part of the share it is about

They are visible to anyone who can open the share. While a comment waits for approval, only you and the owner see it. The owner can hide or delete comments, and they are deleted when the share is deleted. On a private share, the name and text are encrypted in your browser with the share password.

</Def>
<Def term="Votes and reports">Marking a comment helpful, or reporting it to the share’s owner, stores the hashed visitor key against that comment.</Def>
<Def term="Images from other sites">A share page shows each bookmark’s site icon and preview image, and a collection’s logo, by loading them straight from where they are kept, the way any web page loads a picture. That is usually the bookmarked site itself, or Google’s favicon service for a collection logo picked by its domain. Those sites see your IP address and the request, as they would if you visited them. Your browser sends them no referrer, so they aren’t told which share you are looking at. This applies to private shares too, once you unlock one.</Def>
<Def term="IP address">Used only to limit how often comments, votes and first shares from a new code can come from one address. It is not saved to our database.</Def>
</Defs>
</Section>

<Section anchor="owner" title="What you control as an owner">
<Prose>

From the extension you control each share you make:

</Prose>
<Defs>
<Def term="Revoke">Stops the link working straight away.</Def>
<Def term="Delete">Removes the share from our server, with all of its comments, votes and reports.</Def>
<Def term="Expiry">A date after which the link stops working.</Def>
<Def term="View limit">The number of views after which the link stops working. A visitor usually counts once a day.</Def>
<Def term="Comment mode">Open, approve first, or off.</Def>
<Def term="Moderation">Pin, hide or delete comments, and lock the discussion.</Def>
<Def term="Blocked words">On public shares, comments containing a word you block wait for your approval. The list is stored on our server with your code’s record.</Def>
</Defs>
<Prose>

When a share is revoked, expires or reaches its view limit, its link stops working. Its data stays on our server until the share is deleted. Deleting a share deletes its contents and all of its comments, votes and reports.

</Prose>
</Section>

<Section anchor="permissions" title="Permissions">
<Prose>

Chrome lists the permissions an extension asks for. This is what each one is for.

</Prose>
<Defs>
<Def term="Unlimited storage">Keeps a large library on your device without Chrome trimming it.</Def>
<Def term="Active tab">Reads the address and title of the tab you are on when you click the toolbar button.</Def>
<Def term="Scripting">Reads the description and preview image of the page you are saving, once, when you save it.</Def>
<Def term="Tabs">Lets the side panel and full screen offer to save the tab in front, and lets shortcuts switch to a site that is already open.</Def>
<Def term="Side panel, context menus">Show Jupiter Jots in Chrome’s side panel, and add Save page and Save link to the right-click menu.</Def>
<Def term="Favicon">Shows site icons from Chrome’s own icon cache.</Def>
<Def term="Tab groups" aside="(optional)">Asked for only when you open a collection as a tab group (to give it the collection's name and colour) or ask Tab sweep to show your tab groups' names (so each can be saved as a collection).</Def>
<Def term="Bookmarks" aside="(optional)">Asked for only when you import from Chrome. Read once, never changed.</Def>
<Def term="All sites" aside="(optional, off)">Only if you turn on Read descriptions from any tab, check for broken links, or show page descriptions in Tab sweep. Used to read a page’s description and preview image, or to visit a saved address once to see if it still loads.</Def>
<Def term="Tab sweep">Lists the tabs you have open so you can save and close them in one go. It reads their addresses and titles through the Tabs permission and closes only the ones you choose.</Def>
<Def term="Microphone" aside="(asked on first use)">Only for speak-to-type on notes, and only while the mic button is lit.</Def>
</Defs>

<Section anchor="deleting" title="Keeping and deleting your data">
<Prose>

Your library exists only on your device, so **uninstalling Jupiter Jots or clearing its site data deletes everything**. We have no copy to restore it from.

To keep a copy, use **Export** or **Back up** on the Import & export page. A JSON backup can be imported again on any computer. You can also delete single bookmarks, empty the trash or clear the activity log at any time.

Shares are the exception: uninstalling does not remove them from our server. Revoke a share to stop its link. To remove a share and its comments from our server, delete it from the extension’s Shares page (or ask us through [Support](/support) with its link). Visitors can clear the `jj_visitor` cookie and the site’s storage in their browser at any time.

</Prose>
</Section>

Jupiter Jots asks no one, including children under 13, for an email, an account or other personal information. It is a general-purpose tool and is not directed at children. Comments on a share can be seen by anyone with its link, so please don’t post personal details in them.

</Prose>
